## Business
Name: Mesa
Description: Mesa lets people play physical Magic: The Gathering with friends who are somewhere else. A phone camera over the table reads the real cards as they are played and mirrors them onto a shared digital table, so everyone sees the same board while each player's hand and library stay private.
Category: B2C gaming / tabletop-card-game companion app (web app, runs in the phone or desktop browser)
Model: Free during private beta. Pricing is not decided yet. Sign-in is required (accounts are needed for decks, games and the camera features).

## Product
Summary: Players keep playing with their own physical cards. Mesa watches the table through the phone camera, recognises each card, and keeps a live digital copy of the board for everyone in the game. Players without a camera, or who prefer it, can play the same table fully digitally.
Features Summary:
- Mirror my table: the camera reads physical cards (names, tapped/untapped, moves between zones) and mirrors them to the shared table
- Digital table: the same shared board with no camera, cards placed by hand
- Private hand and library per player; hidden information is never shown to opponents
- Deck builder and deck library: add a deck by photographing the deck, pasting a list, or searching cards
- Start a game and invite friends with a code or a link; the host starts the game
- Life totals, counters, tokens, graveyard and other zones
- Name correction: when the camera is unsure, the player is asked to name the card, and the correction is kept
Pricing Strategy: Not decided. Free in private beta.

## Metrics
Key Metrics: games started and completed, players per game, session length, share of cards the camera names correctly without help, corrections per 100 cards, wrong names per 100 cards, repeat games per player.
North Star: Games finished by a group of players who did not need help from the team.
Input Metrics: sign-ins, decks added, games started, players joining a started game, camera enabled in a game, cards recognised, cards corrected by the player (Card Name Changed), cards added by hand (Card Added By Hand), cards removed (Card Removed).

## Customer Journey
Overview:
- Awareness -> Sign-in -> Add a deck -> Set up the table -> Start or join a game -> Play a full game -> Return for another game
Goals by Stage:
- Awareness: understand that physical cards can be played remotely
- Sign-in: get in quickly with a Google account
- Add a deck: get the deck into Mesa in minutes
- Set up the table: place the phone so the camera sees the table, confirm the first card
- Start or join: get everyone into the same game
- Play: the camera keeps up without the players having to manage it
- Return: play again without redoing the setup
Pain Points by Stage:
- Deck: photographing or typing a whole deck feels like work
- Set up the table: phone position, lighting and glare affect how well cards are read
- Play: a card that is misnamed or missed breaks trust; piles of cards (graveyard, library) are hard to read
- Join: friends need an account before they can sit down
Touchpoints by Stage:
- Web app on phone and desktop, invite links and game codes

## Customer Motivation
Objectives Summary: Play real cards with remote friends without giving up the feel of the physical game and without hand-entering every move.
Functional Goals: play a full game of Magic remotely; keep hidden information hidden; not manage a digital interface during play.
Emotional Goals: feel like everyone is at the same table.
Social Goals: keep a regular game night going with friends who live elsewhere.
Reason to Change: video calls cannot keep hidden information, and fully digital clients drop the physical cards the players own and love.
Attractiveness of Solution: keep using the cards you own, with the game state handled for you.
Objections or Fears: camera accuracy, privacy of a camera pointed at the home, lag, setup effort.
Habits or Inertia: playing in person, or on a video call with a webcam pointed at the table.

## Customer Profile
Ideal Profile Description: Magic: The Gathering players who own physical cards and play with a regular group of friends, where some of the group is remote.
Key Segments: friends and family who play Magic remotely; Commander (multiplayer) groups; players who want to keep their physical collection.

## Value
Proposition Summary: Play with your real cards against friends anywhere, with the table and game state mirrored automatically and your hand kept private.
Feature to Value Examples:
- Camera reads cards -> no manual bookkeeping during play
- Private hand and library -> fair play over distance
- Shared digital table -> everyone sees the same board
- Deck library -> set up once, play many games

## Growth Model
Acquisition: invitation by existing players; Magic communities.
Activation: first full game played in a group.
Retention: regular game nights with the same group.
Referral: every game needs several players, so each player invites the others.
Monetization: not decided.

## Competitive Advantage
Summary: Works with physical cards through the phone camera, in the browser, with no hardware.
Counter Positioning: digital Magic clients replace the physical cards; video calls cannot hide each player's hand.
Network Effects: a group plays together, so each added player brings the group.
Switching Costs: low for now; decks and game history build up over time.
Brand Strength: early stage, private beta.

## Strategy
OKR Objectives:
- Objective: Prove that strangers can play a whole game on Mesa without help
  - Key Results:
    - Two groups outside the team (at least 6 players) each finish a full game
    - Camera mistakes stay low enough that players trust the table (tracked as wrong names per 100 cards)
    - Players come back for a second game
