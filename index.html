    Read the board from a picture

    Paste a screenshot of a video call and Mesa finds each player's pane and reads the cards on their table — one player per pane. A cropped picture of one or more cards works too: those get added to your hand.

    Press ⌘V anywhere in the app, or drag in an image file.
    Pasting later adds the cards — your hand is only replaced if you re-read the board in the image view.

      Min lek

      Kameran känner igen kort mot din lek — ett hundratal kandidater i stället för trettiotusen. Det är skillnaden mellan igenkänning som fungerar och en ström av osäkra kort.

      Så fotograferar du leken

      1. Lägg korten i högar bredvid varandra, överlappande nedåt, så att bara titelraden syns på alla utom det nedersta i varje hög.
      2. Fotografera rakt uppifrån i jämnt ljus. Ungefär trettio kort per foto — fler än så blir titelraderna för små att läsa.
      3. Lägg nästa trettio på bordet och fotografera igen. Leken växer för varje foto tills den är slut.

      Basländerna fotograferar du inte. Dem fyller du i som antal när listan börjat växa — kameran känner ändå igen ett Mountain på symbolen, och letar bland tjugofyra tryckningar av varje landskap oavsett vilken du äger.

      Du kan också släppa fotot här i rutan, eller klistra in det med ⌘V. Öppnar du Mesa i telefonen fotograferar du direkt.

        Paste card names

        One card name per line. A count in front works (2 Sol Ring, 1x Llanowar Elves), as does a set suffix from decklists (Sol Ring (LTC) 268). Typos are corrected automatically where possible.

        This pane

        How Mesa reads a screenshot: it first looks for the video panes (black gaps separate them), then for dark card borders against the light table in each pane, and finally matches every hit against the set's image signatures. Only cards that match clearly are added automatically — the rest end up in the confirm row at the top.

        Matching happens against a limited set (⋯ → Card pool). That is what makes it work: 780 cards can be told apart visually, all 30,000 MTG cards cannot. Card data and images come from Scryfall. Everything is stored in this browser only.

        Card pool to match against

        Scanning matches against a limited set — that is why it works. 780 cards can be told apart visually; 30 000 cannot. Pick the set you are playing with.

        No pool selectedPick a set above.

        The index is built once per set: ~780 card images are fetched from Scryfall, get a 528-byte image signature, and are stored locally. Takes about half a minute and never has to be redone.

        AI help with uncertain cards

        Local recognition handles most cards on its own. For the ones it isn't sure about, an image model can decide — it sees the cropped card and the best candidates, and picks one.

        The app is running without a server, so AI help needs your own key from console.anthropic.com. The key is stored in this browser and only sent to Anthropic — but anyone with access to this computer can read it, so use a separate key with a spending limit.
